## Why convert a PDF to slides?

People often receive presentations as PDFs — conference talks, client decks, course material. Converting back to PowerPoint helps when you need to:

- **Present it** with PowerPoint's presenter view, a clicker and full-screen slides.
- **Reuse a few slides** in your own deck.
- **Update a figure or a date** on a slide you no longer have the source for.
- **Turn a document into a presentation** — a report page per slide to walk through in a meeting.

## What to expect

| PDF content | In PowerPoint |
|---|---|
| Slide decks exported to PDF | One slide per page, looking like the original |
| Simple text blocks | Editable where the text can be extracted |
| Charts, diagrams, photos | Kept visually as part of the slide |
| Animations, transitions, notes | Not present in the PDF, so not recoverable |
| Scanned pages | Slide images only, no editable text |

## Common problems and fixes

**I can't edit some of the text.** Text that is part of an image in the PDF — a screenshot, a logo, a scanned slide — stays part of that image. Retype it in a new text box on the slide.

**Slides have an unusual size.** Slides take the proportions of the PDF pages. A presentation exported to PDF keeps its 16:9 or 4:3 shape; an A4 document produces portrait slides. Change the slide size in PowerPoint's *Design* menu if needed.

**The file is large.** Each slide includes a picture of its page, so long documents with many images produce heavy decks. Convert only the pages you need.

**Fonts look different after editing.** Fonts used in the PDF may not be installed on your computer, so PowerPoint shows a replacement when you edit the text.
