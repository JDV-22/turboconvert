## AVIF in a nutshell

AVIF (AV1 Image File Format) is one of the newest image formats on the web. Built on the AV1 video codec, it can store photos in noticeably smaller files than JPG and often smaller than WebP, supports transparency and HDR, and is now displayed by all major browsers. Websites and image delivery networks increasingly serve AVIF automatically — so a picture you save from a page may arrive as .avif even if you expected a JPG.

Outside the browser, support is still patchy. Older versions of Windows Photos, Photoshop, Office, many print services and most upload forms won't accept the file.

| | AVIF | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compression efficiency | Very high | High | Standard |
| Transparency | Yes | Yes | No |
| Support in apps and forms | Limited | Partial | Universal |

## When to convert

- **Uploading to a form or platform** that only accepts JPG or PNG.
- **Editing in software** that can't open AVIF.
- **Printing** through an online print service.
- **Sending to someone** whose device or email client may not display AVIF.

## Common problems

- **"The file can't be read."** Your browser may be too old to decode AVIF. Update it, or try another current browser such as Chrome or Firefox.
- **The colors look slightly different.** Some AVIF images use wide-gamut or HDR color, which is mapped to standard colors in a JPG. For most images the difference is negligible.
- **A logo has a white background now.** JPG doesn't support transparency; export a PNG instead as explained in the FAQ.
