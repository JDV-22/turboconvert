## Sign without printing or scanning

Printing a document just to sign it, scanning it and emailing it back takes ten minutes and a printer. Here it takes under a minute: open the PDF, create your signature once and click where it goes. The result is a normal PDF you can send by email, upload to an online portal or archive.

## Three ways to create your signature

- **Draw** — with a mouse, trackpad, stylus or finger. Choose black or blue ink. The empty space around it is cropped automatically.
- **Type** — enter your name and it's rendered in a handwriting-style font installed on your device.
- **Upload image** — a photo or scan of your real signature, in PNG, JPG or WebP.

Once created, the Signature tool stays active: click on each page that needs signing or initialling. Add **Date** and **Text** (for "Read and approved", your name or the place of signature) from the toolbar.

## Is an electronic signature valid?

A signature added with this tool is a **simple electronic signature**. Under the EU eIDAS regulation it cannot be refused as evidence just because it's electronic, and under US law (ESIGN Act and UETA) electronic signatures generally have the same legal effect as handwritten ones. In practice it's accepted for the vast majority of everyday documents: rental agreements, quotes and purchase orders, employment and school forms, delivery confirmations.

Some situations require an **advanced or qualified** signature, based on a certificate that identifies you — for example notarial acts or certain public procurement. Those are issued by trust service providers and can't be produced by any free in-browser tool. When a document specifies this, follow the sender's instructions.

## Private by design

Signed documents are often sensitive: contracts, IDs, bank details. Online signing services usually upload them to their servers. This page doesn't: the PDF is displayed and saved by code running in your browser tab, and your signature exists only in that tab until you close it.
