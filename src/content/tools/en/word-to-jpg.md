## When a page works better as an image

A Word file needs an app to open; a JPG shows up everywhere instantly. Converting pages to images is handy for:

- **Social media** — share a poem, a quote, a flyer or an announcement as a post.
- **Messaging apps** — WhatsApp, Messenger and Slack show images inline, so people read without opening a file.
- **Websites and blogs** — embed a document page as a preview.
- **Presentations** — drop a page of a report into a slide.
- **Thumbnails** — a cover image for a downloadable document.

## Tips

- **Design for the screen.** For social posts, large fonts and short text read best; a dense A4 page is hard to read on a phone.
- **Images too heavy for your site?** Shrink them with [Compress image](/compress-image) or resize them to the exact width needed.
- **Use common fonts** if the exact look matters — unavailable fonts are substituted.

Your document is processed locally in your browser: nothing is uploaded, nothing is stored.

## Common problems and fixes

**"Not a supported file."** The document is probably an old .doc, an .odt or a .pages file. Save or export it as .docx first — Word, LibreOffice, Google Docs and Pages all offer this.

**The text looks slightly different from Word.** A font that is not available to the browser has been replaced by a similar one. Use a widely installed font in the document if the exact look matters.

**I need higher resolution for printing.** Convert the document with Word to PDF, then use PDF to JPG with the Print (300 dpi) setting — that route lets you choose the resolution and quality.

**I only need the first page.** Download just that image from the results list instead of the ZIP. A one-page document downloads automatically.

**Images inside the document look soft.** The page is rendered as a whole; pictures that were inserted small or at low resolution in the document stay that way. Insert a higher-resolution picture in Word and convert again.

## Posting on social media

For Instagram and similar platforms, square or portrait images work best. A portrait A4 or Letter page fits reasonably well in a portrait post; landscape pages are better suited to LinkedIn or X. Keep important text away from the edges, which some apps crop in previews.
