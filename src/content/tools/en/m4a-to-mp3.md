## Why convert M4A to MP3?

M4A with AAC is an excellent format — often slightly better quality than MP3 at the same size — and Apple devices use it everywhere. But MP3 is still the most universally accepted audio format. Converting helps when:

- **A car stereo, older MP3 player or smart speaker** doesn't recognise M4A.
- **A website, podcast host or upload form** only accepts MP3.
- **Someone on Windows or Android** can't open the voice memo you sent.
- **Transcription or editing software** expects MP3.

## Choosing the quality

| Audio quality | Size per minute | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 128 kbps | ≈ 1 MB | Voice memos, interviews, lectures |
| 192 kbps | ≈ 1.4 MB | Everyday listening (default) |
| 256 kbps | ≈ 1.9 MB | iTunes music (purchased at 256 kbps AAC) |
| 320 kbps | ≈ 2.4 MB | Maximum MP3 quality |

Voice memos are speech, so 128 kbps gives small, clear files. For music, match or exceed the source bitrate.

## Common problems

- **"The file can't be converted."** It may be protected by DRM (.m4p or Apple Music downloads). Only unprotected files can be converted.
- **The MP3 is bigger than the M4A.** AAC is more efficient than MP3, so the MP3 may need a higher bitrate to sound the same. For speech, 128 kbps keeps files small.

Several files at once? Drop a whole folder of voice memos or an album; they're converted one after another with the same quality setting.
