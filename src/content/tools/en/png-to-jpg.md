## PNG or JPG: which one should you use?

**PNG** is a lossless format: perfect for screenshots, logos, diagrams and anything with transparency or sharp edges. The price is size — a PNG photo can easily weigh 5 to 15 MB.

**JPG** is designed for photographs. It discards detail the eye barely notices, which makes files far smaller and universally compatible: every phone, email client, website builder and printer accepts JPG.

| | PNG | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | Lossless | Lossy (adjustable) |
| Transparency | Yes | No |
| Typical photo size | 5–15 MB | 0.5–2 MB |
| Best for | Screenshots, logos, graphics | Photos, sharing, email |

## When converting to JPG makes sense

- **Email and messaging** — attachment limits are easier to respect with JPG.
- **Websites and online forms** — many upload forms only accept JPG, or limit file size.
- **Photo printing services** — JPG is the standard format.

## Choosing the right quality

The quality slider controls the trade-off between size and detail. As a rule of thumb, **92%** is indistinguishable from the original, **80–85%** is ideal for the web, and below **70%** compression artifacts start to appear around edges. Want an even lighter file?
