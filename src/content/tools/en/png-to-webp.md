## Why convert PNG to WebP?

PNG is perfect for graphics with transparency, but it's lossless, so files are often far larger than they need to be on a website — especially photos or detailed illustrations saved as PNG. WebP supports the same transparency with much more efficient compression, which means faster pages and less bandwidth.

Common uses:

- **Product photos with transparent backgrounds** for online shops.
- **Logos, badges and illustrations** on landing pages.
- **Screenshots** in documentation and blog posts.
- **Sticker and icon sets** for web apps.

## PNG vs WebP

| | PNG | WebP |
|---|---|---|
| Transparency | Yes | Yes |
| Compression | Lossless only | Lossy or lossless |
| Typical size | Large | Much smaller |
| Browser support | Universal | All current browsers |
| Best for | Editing, print, master files | Publishing on the web |

Keep your PNG originals as master files and publish the WebP versions.

## Getting the quality right

The **Quality** slider defaults to 82%, a good value for photographic content. Graphics with flat colors and sharp edges are more sensitive: if you see slight blurring around text or fine lines, convert again at 90–95%. The file will still usually be lighter than the PNG.

If a PNG is a photo without transparency, [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg) is another option for places where WebP isn't accepted, such as some email clients.

## Common problems

- **Text looks fuzzy in the WebP.** Raise the quality to 90–95% for screenshots and graphics.
- **The WebP isn't accepted by an app.** Some desktop software and email clients still don't read WebP. Keep the PNG for those, or use [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg) when transparency isn't needed.

## Private and fast

Your PNGs are converted by your browser's built-in image engine on your device. There's no upload, so large batches go quickly and unreleased designs stay private.
