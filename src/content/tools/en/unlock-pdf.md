## Two kinds of PDF passwords

| Type | What you see | What this tool does |
|---|---|---|
| **Open password** (user password) | The PDF asks for a password before showing anything | Removes it — you must type the correct password once |
| **Permissions password** (owner password) | The PDF opens, but printing, copying or editing is disabled | Removes the restrictions — no password needed |

Banks, payroll services and phone companies often send statements protected with an open password such as your date of birth or part of your account number. Once you have opened it, there is no need to type it every time.

## When unlocking is useful

- **Before merging or compressing.** Encrypted PDFs are rejected by most PDF tools, including [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) and [PDF to Word](/pdf-to-word).
- **For archiving.** Years later, nobody remembers which password went with which statement.
- **For printing or copying text** from a document you are entitled to use.

## Be careful with the unlocked copy

An unlocked PDF can be opened by anyone who gets hold of it. Store it in a safe place, and if you need to send it on, protect it again with your own password using [Protect PDF](/protect-pdf).

The whole process happens in your browser: neither the document nor its password is sent anywhere.

## Common problems and fixes

**"Wrong password."** Passwords are case-sensitive. For bank and payroll statements, check the format they told you — for example a date of birth as DDMMYYYY rather than DD/MM/YYYY, or the last digits of an account number.

**The PDF still asks for a password.** Make sure you are opening the new copy from your downloads folder, not the original file, which is left unchanged.

**I don't know the password at all.** Ask the sender — banks, employers and schools can tell you the format or send an unprotected copy. TurboConvert doesn't try to guess passwords.

**The PDF opens, but my editor still refuses to change it.** Leave the password field empty and convert: this removes printing, copying and editing restrictions. Note that some files are also digitally signed — editing a signed PDF invalidates the signature, whether or not it was restricted.

## Is it legal to unlock a PDF?

Removing a password from your own documents, or from files you have the right to use — your bank statements, payslips, documents a colleague shared with you — is a normal, everyday task. Don't use it to bypass protection on documents you are not authorised to access or modify.
