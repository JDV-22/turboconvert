## When you need a PDF as an image

A PDF is ideal for documents, but many places only accept images:

- **Social media and messaging** — Instagram, WhatsApp statuses and most chat apps show images inline but not PDFs.
- **Presentations and websites** — dropping a page into PowerPoint, Keynote, Canva or a blog post is easier as a JPG.
- **Upload forms** — some portals ask for a JPG of a certificate, ID or proof of address.
- **Previews and thumbnails** — a cover image of a report or an e-book.

## Choosing the right resolution

Resolution (dpi, dots per inch) decides how many pixels each page gets. For a standard page:

| Resolution | A4 page in pixels | Letter page in pixels | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Screen (72 dpi)** | about 595 × 842 | 612 × 792 | Thumbnails, previews, quick sharing |
| **Standard (150 dpi)** | about 1240 × 1754 | 1275 × 1650 | Screens, slides, websites, messaging |
| **Print (300 dpi)** | about 2480 × 3508 | 2550 × 3300 | Printing, zooming into small text |

Doubling the dpi multiplies the number of pixels by four, so a 300 dpi page is roughly four times heavier than the same page at 150 dpi. If you are not sure, Standard is the safe choice.

## Quality setting

The **Quality** slider controls JPG compression. The default (90%) is visually indistinguishable from the original for most pages. Lower it to 75–80% if you need lighter images for a website; keep it high for pages with small text, where heavy compression creates fuzzy halos around letters.

## JPG or PNG?

| | JPG | PNG |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | Lossy (adjustable) | Lossless |
| File size | Small | Larger, especially for photos |
| Best for | Pages with photos, brochures, slides | Text, diagrams, charts, line drawings |
| Compatibility | Universal | Universal |

For a scanned document or a photo-rich brochure, JPG is the better choice. For a technical drawing or a page of fine print, use PNG.

## Common problems and fixes

**The images look blurry when I zoom in.** The resolution is too low for how you are using them. Convert again with Print (300 dpi) — especially for pages with small text, maps or technical drawings.

**The files are too large.** A 300 dpi page at high quality can weigh a few megabytes. For the web, chat apps and slides, Standard (150 dpi) with quality around 80% is usually more than enough.

**I get a ZIP full of pages I don't need.** Split the PDF first and convert only the pages you want, or download individual images from the results list instead of the ZIP.

**"This PDF is password-protected."** A PDF that asks for a password to open must be unlocked before its pages can be rendered.

**Colours look slightly different.** PDFs prepared for professional printing sometimes use CMYK colours, while screens and JPG files use RGB. A small shift in very saturated colours is normal; everyday documents are not affected.
