## Why share a spreadsheet as PDF?

An Excel file invites edits, depends on the recipient having a spreadsheet app, and can look completely different on a phone. A PDF is read-only, prints the same everywhere and opens on any device. Typical uses:

- **Invoices, quotes and timesheets** built in Excel and sent to clients.
- **Price lists and schedules** shared with people who just need to read them.
- **Reports and budgets** attached to an email or printed for a meeting.
- **CSV exports** from apps and databases that need to be readable by humans.

## Tips for a readable PDF

- **Hide or delete helper columns** and scratch sheets before converting; everything that is in the file will be printed.
- **Keep headers in the first row** so the table is easy to read on every page.
- **Very wide sheet?** Consider splitting it into two logical tables, or landscape plus narrower columns.
- **Combine with other documents.** Convert a Word cover letter with [Word to PDF](/word-to-pdf), then join both with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf).
- **Need to edit the data again from a PDF?** [PDF to Excel](/pdf-to-excel) extracts tables back into a spreadsheet.

Your spreadsheet is read and converted inside your browser — salaries, client lists and financial models are never uploaded.

## Common problems and fixes

**The text is tiny.** A sheet with dozens of columns has to be scaled down to fit a page. Remove columns nobody needs, or split the data into two sheets, before converting.

**A figure looks out of date.** The PDF shows the values as they were last saved. Open the workbook in Excel, Google Sheets or LibreOffice, let it recalculate, save, and convert again.

**My chart is missing.** Charts are not drawn in the PDF. Copy the chart as a picture into a Word document and convert that with Word to PDF, then merge the two PDFs.

**Strange characters in a CSV.** CSV files exported by older software sometimes use a legacy encoding. Open the file in a spreadsheet app, save it as XLSX, and convert that instead.

**Empty pages in the PDF.** Cells that look empty can still contain spaces or formatting far below your data. Delete the unused rows and columns, save, and convert again.

## XLSX, XLS, CSV or ODS?

**XLSX** is the modern Excel format and the most reliable input. **CSV** is plain data with no formatting — ideal for exports from apps and databases. Older **XLS** and OpenDocument **ODS** files are accepted as well; if anything looks off, save them as XLSX first.
