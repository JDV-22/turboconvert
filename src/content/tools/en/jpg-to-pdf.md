## Why turn images into a PDF?

A PDF keeps several images together in a fixed order, in one file that every computer, phone and online form can open. Typical reasons:

- **Online applications and forms** — job applications, visas, rental files, insurance claims and university portals often accept only PDF, and sometimes only *one* file.
- **Receipts and expense reports** — photograph each receipt and send them as a single document.
- **Scanned homework or signed pages** — phone photos of paper pages become a document that reads like a scan.
- **Sharing a set of photos** — one attachment instead of fifteen, always in the right order.

## Choosing page size and margins

| Setting | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Same as image** | Each page takes the exact size and orientation of its image | Viewing on screen, photo sets, mixed portrait and landscape |
| **A4** | Standard page used in most countries; the image is fitted inside | Printing, official forms (Europe, Asia, Africa…) |
| **Letter** | US and Canada standard page size | Printing and forms in North America |
| **Margin: None** | Image goes edge to edge | Photos, screenshots |
| **Margin: Small / Large** | Adds white space around the image | Documents to print or annotate |

With A4 or Letter, images keep their proportions — they are never stretched.

## JPG, PNG, HEIC: all accepted

Despite the name, the tool accepts most image formats and lets you mix them:

- **JPG / JPEG** — photos from cameras, Android phones and the web.
- **HEIC / HEIF** — the default photo format on iPhone. No need to convert to JPG first.
- **PNG** — screenshots and graphics. There is also a dedicated [PNG to PDF](/png-to-pdf) page.
- **WebP, AVIF, GIF, BMP** — images saved from websites and older software.

## Tips for a clean, professional PDF

- **Name your files in order** (`01.jpg`, `02.jpg`…) before selecting them. They are added in that order, which saves reordering.
- **Photographing paper pages?** Shoot from directly above in good light, fill the frame with the page and avoid shadows. Crop the photos before converting for a scan-like result.
- **Mind the file size.** Modern phone photos are several megabytes each, so twenty of them can make a PDF too large for email. Compress the finished PDF, or reduce the photos first with [Resize image](/resize-image).
- **Need to add pages later?** Convert the new images, then join both PDFs with Merge PDF.
- **Going the other way?** To get images back out of a PDF, use [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg).

## Common problems and fixes

**The pages are in the wrong order.** Images are added in the order you select them — on some phones that is the order you tapped, on computers it is often alphabetical. Use the arrows to fix the order before clicking Convert.

**A photo appears sideways.** Some cameras store the orientation as a tag rather than rotating the pixels. If a page still comes out sideways, fix it in seconds with [Rotate PDF](/rotate-pdf), or rotate the photo in your gallery and convert again.

**The PDF is too large to send.** Phone photos of 3–5 MB each add up fast. Compress the finished PDF — most upload portals are satisfied with the Recommended level.

**"Not a supported file."** RAW camera files, PDFs and documents can't be used as images here. Export RAW photos to JPG first; to combine existing PDFs, use Merge PDF instead.

**White borders around photos.** With A4 or Letter, a photo whose proportions differ from the page leaves white space. Choose *Same as image* for borderless pages, or keep A4 with no margin for the thinnest borders.
