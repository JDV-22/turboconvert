## Why send a PDF instead of a Word file?

A Word document can look different on someone else's computer: other fonts, another version of Word, a different page size or a phone app that ignores half the formatting. A PDF fixes the layout so that every recipient sees the same pages. It is the expected format for:

- **CVs and cover letters** — recruiters and applicant tracking systems widely accept PDF, and your layout stays intact.
- **Invoices, quotes and contracts** — a PDF is harder to modify by accident and prints identically everywhere.
- **School and university submissions** — many portals ask for PDF only.
- **Anything you print** — print shops and office printers handle PDF most reliably.

## Word to PDF vs. "Save as PDF" in Word

If you have Microsoft Word on a computer, *File › Save As › PDF* is a good option and uses your installed fonts. TurboConvert is useful when that is not at hand:

## Tips for a perfect PDF

- **Stick to common fonts** for documents that must look exact. Decorative or brand fonts that are not available in the browser are substituted.
- **Check page breaks.** If one line spills onto a new page, slightly reduce spacing or margins in the document and convert again.
- **Compress after converting.** Documents with many photos make heavy PDFs. Run the result through [Compress PDF](/compress-pdf) before emailing it.
- **Combining several documents?** Convert each one, then join them with [Merge PDF](/merge-pdf) — a cover letter and CV in one file, for example.
- **Need an image instead?** For a page you want to post or embed, [Word to JPG](/word-to-jpg) saves each page as a picture.
- **Other Office files?** Spreadsheets and slides have their own converters: [Excel to PDF](/excel-to-pdf) and PowerPoint to PDF.

## Common problems

**"Not a supported file."** The file is probably a .doc, .odt or .rtf. Save it as .docx first.

**Layout shifts slightly.** Usually a missing font. Change the font to a common one, or accept the substitute if the result looks fine.

**The PDF is too large.** Images inserted at full camera resolution are the usual cause. Compress the PDF, or reduce the images in Word (*Compress Pictures*) before converting.

**Text near the bottom of a page moved to the next page.** A substituted font can be slightly wider than the original, which pushes the last lines over. Tighten the spacing a little, or use a widely available font, and convert again.

**Tracked changes or comments are still in the document.** Accept or reject tracked changes and delete comments in Word before converting, so the PDF shows only the clean final text you intend to send.
