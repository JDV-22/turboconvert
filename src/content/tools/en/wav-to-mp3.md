## WAV vs MP3

**WAV** stores audio uncompressed — every sample exactly as recorded. That's what recorders, DAWs (Ableton, Logic, FL Studio, Audacity) and CD rips produce, and it's ideal for editing. But it's heavy: about 10 MB per minute at CD quality, much more for 24-bit or high-sample-rate recordings.

**MP3** uses perceptual compression to drop what the ear is least likely to notice. The result is a file that plays on every phone, car stereo, website and app, at a fraction of the size.

| | WAV | MP3 |
|---|---|---|
| Compression | None (lossless) | Lossy, adjustable |
| Size per minute (CD quality) | ≈ 10 MB | ≈ 1–2.4 MB |
| Best for | Recording, editing, mastering | Sharing, streaming, listening |
| Compatibility | Very good | Universal |

## Choosing the bitrate

| Audio quality | Size per minute | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 128 kbps | ≈ 1 MB | Voice memos, podcasts, lectures |
| 192 kbps | ≈ 1.4 MB | Everyday listening (default) |
| 256 kbps | ≈ 1.9 MB | Music |
| 320 kbps | ≈ 2.4 MB | Music demos, DJ sets, archiving for listening |

## Typical uses

- **Sending demos** to a band, a label or a client by email or messaging.
- **Publishing podcasts** — hosting platforms usually expect MP3.
- **Putting recordings on a phone** or a USB stick for the car.
- **Saving space** on a large library of voice recordings or field recordings.

## Common problems

- **The MP3 sounds slightly different from the WAV.** At 128 kbps, cymbals and reverb tails can lose detail. Use 256 or 320 kbps for music.
- **Large 24-bit or multichannel WAVs.** These are accepted up to 1 GB per file; very long recordings convert faster on a computer.
