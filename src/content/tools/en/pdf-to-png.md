## When PNG is the right format

PNG uses lossless compression: every pixel is stored exactly. That makes it the format of choice when you convert pages that contain:

- **Text and documentation** — screenshots for a manual, a knowledge base or a support ticket.
- **Charts, graphs and slides** — flat colours and thin lines stay crisp.
- **Technical drawings, plans and diagrams** — no compression artifacts around fine lines.
- **Images you will edit further** — in Photoshop, GIMP, Figma or Canva, without losing quality on every save.

## Resolution and size

| Resolution | A4 page size in pixels | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| Screen (72 dpi) | about 595 × 842 | Thumbnails, previews |
| Standard (150 dpi) | about 1240 × 1754 | Screens, documents, web |
| Print (300 dpi) | about 2480 × 3508 | Printing, close zooming |

At 300 dpi each image has four times as many pixels as at 150 dpi, so files are much larger. For long documents, Standard is usually the best balance.

## PNG or JPG?

If a page is mostly a photograph, a PNG can be several times heavier than a JPG of the same page without looking any better. A good rule: **text and graphics → PNG, photos → JPG**. If you end up with PNGs that are too heavy for the web, convert them with [PNG to JPG](/png-to-jpg) or [PNG to WebP](/png-to-webp).

## Common problems and fixes

**The PNG files are huge.** The page contains a large photo or a scan, which PNG stores without compression losses. Lower the resolution to Standard, or switch to [PDF to JPG](/pdf-to-jpg) for those pages.

**Small text is not sharp enough.** 72 dpi is meant for thumbnails. Use Standard for screens and Print (300 dpi) when people will zoom in or print the image.

**My browser struggles with a long document.** Rendering hundreds of pages at 300 dpi uses a lot of memory. Convert in smaller batches by splitting the PDF first, or use a computer rather than a phone.

**"This PDF is password-protected."** Unlock the PDF with its password first, then convert the unlocked copy.

**Only one page is useful.** Download that image from the results list rather than the whole ZIP — each page has its own Download button.
