## What is WebP, and why convert it?

WebP is an image format created by Google for the web. It handles both photos (lossy, like JPG) and graphics (lossless, like PNG), supports transparency and animation, and typically produces files 25–35% smaller than JPG at a similar quality. That's why so many websites now serve their images as WebP.

The catch appears once the image is on your computer. Older versions of Photoshop and Microsoft Office, many print services, some messaging and social apps, and plenty of online forms still don't accept WebP. A JPG, on the other hand, opens everywhere.

| | WebP | JPG |
|---|---|---|
| Typical size for a photo | Smaller | About 25–35% larger |
| Transparency | Yes | No |
| Animation | Yes | No |
| Opens in older software and upload forms | Not always | Yes |

## Why your browser saved a WebP

You right-click an image, choose *Save image as…*, and get a .webp file. That's not your browser's choice: the website checks which formats your browser understands and sends the lightest one. The same address can deliver a JPG to an old browser and a WebP to a modern one. Renaming the file doesn't change what's inside — converting does.

## WebP to JPG or WebP to PNG?

- **Choose JPG** for photos, product pictures, screenshots of web pages and anything you'll print, email or upload to a form. It gives the best balance of compatibility and size.
- **Choose PNG** when the image has a transparent background (logos, icons, stickers) or sharp graphics and text you'll keep editing. See [WebP to PNG](/webp-to-png).

When a WebP with transparency is converted to JPG, the transparent pixels are filled with white, which looks fine for most logos placed on a white page.

## Quality settings

The **Quality** slider starts at 92%, which keeps the JPG visually identical to the WebP. Since most WebP images from the web are already compressed, going higher mostly makes the file bigger without visible benefit. For email or forms with a file size limit, 80–85% is a good compromise; you can also reduce the dimensions afterwards with [Resize image](/resize-image).

## Common problems

- **"The file opens but looks wrong in my editor."** It was probably a renamed WebP. Run it through the converter to get a genuine JPG.
- **A logo got a white box around it.** The WebP had a transparent background, which JPG can't store. Convert it to PNG instead.
- **The JPG is bigger than the WebP.** That's expected — WebP is simply more efficient. Lower the quality to around 80% if size matters, or resize the image.
- **An animation became a still image.** JPG can't animate; only the first frame is kept.
