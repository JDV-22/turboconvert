There is no shortage of free PDF websites, and most of them do the basics — merge, compress, convert — well. The real differences are elsewhere: **how much you can do before hitting a paywall**, **where your file is processed**, and **which advanced tools** (editing, e-signing, OCR) are included. This guide compares the most popular options fairly, including our own, and tells you when each one is the right pick.

*How we compared:* the limits below come from each service's own public pages and help centres, checked in October 2026. Free plans change often — if a number matters to you, check the provider's pricing page before relying on it.

## What to look for in a free PDF tool

- **Usage limits.** Some free tiers cap the number of tasks per day, the size of each file or the number of files per task.
- **Where processing happens.** Most online tools upload your file to a server, process it, then delete it after a set time. Others run on your device (desktop apps, or browser tools built with WebAssembly). Both can be legitimate; the second means your document never leaves your computer.
- **Output quality.** Compression should re-encode images intelligently, not wreck text. Conversions to Word should keep paragraphs editable.
- **The tools you actually need.** Merge, split and compress are everywhere. Editing text, signing, filling forms and OCR are where suites differ.
- **Account and ads.** Having to sign up for a one-off task is friction; heavy ads next to download buttons are a red flag.

## iLovePDF: the all-rounder

iLovePDF covers nearly every PDF task: merge, split, compress, convert to and from Office, OCR, watermark, page numbers, repair, sign and more, with web, desktop and mobile apps. The free tier is usable for occasional tasks, with caps per task published on its pricing page (file size and number of documents); Premium lifts them and adds batch processing and digital signatures.

**Pick it if** you need a broad suite with editing and signing and don't mind files being processed on its servers. **Look elsewhere if** you regularly handle files above the free caps or prefer documents to stay on your machine.

## Smallpdf: polished, but stingy when free

Smallpdf is easy to use and strong on editing, e-signatures and team features. Its free plan is designed for occasional use: Smallpdf's own pages describe daily limits, often two tasks per day, after which you wait 24 hours or upgrade to Pro (a 7-day trial is offered).

**Pick it if** you need a paid all-in-one with collaboration. **A Smallpdf alternative makes sense if** you keep hitting the daily limit for basic tasks like compress or merge — PDF24, TurboConvert or your system's built-in tools do those for free without a counter.

## PDF24: the most generous free suite

PDF24 Tools is free and funded by advertising, with no task counter and a very large set of tools, including OCR. Online processing happens on servers in EU data centres, and PDF24 states that files are deleted automatically within one hour. Its **PDF24 Creator** desktop app is free for private and commercial use, works offline and keeps files on your PC — but it's **Windows only**.

**Pick it if** you're on Windows and want a free offline suite, or need a tool others don't have. **On Mac or a phone**, the online tools work in any browser.

## Adobe Acrobat online: the reference, with tight free limits

Adobe invented PDF, and its PDF-to-Word conversion is among the most faithful. The free online tools, however, are mostly a trial of Acrobat: Adobe's FAQ describes a limited number of free transactions for premium tools, and most require signing in. Acrobat Reader remains free for viewing, filling forms, commenting and signing.

**Pick it if** conversion fidelity on complex documents justifies a subscription. **For free**, use Reader for forms and signatures and another tool for the rest.

## TurboConvert: everything in the browser

Full disclosure: this is our tool. TurboConvert runs every conversion inside your browser using WebAssembly — Ghostscript for PDF compression, pdf-lib for merging and splitting, Tesseract for OCR, FFmpeg for audio and video. Files are never uploaded, there's no account and no daily limit.

What it does well: [compress](/compress-pdf), [merge](/merge-pdf), [split](/split-pdf), [organize](/organize-pdf) and [rotate](/rotate-pdf) PDFs; convert [PDF to Word](/pdf-to-word), [Excel](/pdf-to-excel) and [JPG](/pdf-to-jpg); [protect](/protect-pdf) or [unlock](/unlock-pdf) with a known password; [OCR](/ocr-pdf) in six languages; plus image, audio and video tools that PDF suites don't cover.

What it doesn't do (yet): edit existing text inside a PDF, collect e-signatures, or process very large files — the limit is 100–200 MB per file depending on the tool, and very large jobs depend on your device's memory. Long OCR or video jobs are slower on older phones than on a server.

**Pick it if** privacy matters (contracts, payslips, IDs, medical files) or you're tired of task counters.

## Don't forget the free tools you already have

- **Mac — Preview:** merge PDFs by dragging thumbnails, reorder and delete pages, sign, annotate, fill forms, password-protect (*File > Export > Permissions*) and reduce size. See our guide to [merging PDFs on a Mac](/blog/how-to-merge-pdf-on-mac).
- **Windows — Microsoft Edge:** opens PDFs, fills forms, highlights, adds text and draws. **Microsoft Print to PDF** creates PDFs from any app. Windows has no built-in way to merge or compress PDFs.
- **iPhone/iPad — Files app:** select several PDFs or images and choose *Create PDF* to combine them; Markup lets you sign and annotate.
- **Android:** print from almost any app and choose *Save as PDF*; Google Drive has a document scanner.

## A word on privacy

Server-based tools are not "unsafe" by default: the established ones use encrypted connections and publish deletion policies. The question is whether you're allowed — or comfortable — to send a document to a third party at all. Employment contracts, health records, client files and ID scans often fall under confidentiality rules. For those, local processing (desktop app, built-in app or a browser tool that doesn't upload) removes the question entirely. With TurboConvert you can verify it yourself: open your browser's developer tools, *Network* tab, and watch — your file is never sent.
